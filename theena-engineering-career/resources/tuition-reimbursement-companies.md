# Employers Known for Paying for School

**How to use this list:** it's a starting map, not gospel. Programs change yearly —
before using any number in your Week 4 spreadsheet, **verify it on the company's
current careers/benefits page or in a current job posting.** Also: local mid-size
manufacturers you found in Week 3 often have tuition benefits that never make lists
like this — the only way to find out is to check their careers page or ask in an
interview ("does the company offer education assistance?" is a great question that
also signals ambition).

## Useful background

- The IRS lets employers provide up to **$5,250/year in tax-free tuition assistance**
  (IRC Section 127). That's why so many programs land at exactly that number.
- "Tuition reimbursement" (you pay, they pay you back per grade/semester) is
  different from "tuition paid up front" — up-front programs are much easier
  when you don't have savings. Note which kind each employer offers.
- Many programs restrict field of study to something job-related — engineering
  almost always qualifies at these employers, but confirm.
- Ask about **strings**: minimum tenure before eligibility (often 90 days–1 year),
  GPA requirements, and payback clauses if you quit soon after.

## National employers with well-known education programs (verify current details)

### Directly in the engineering/industrial world
- **Defense & aerospace:** Boeing, Lockheed Martin, Raytheon (RTX), Northrop Grumman, General Dynamics, BAE Systems — historically strong tuition programs and huge technician workforces. They hire entry-level assemblers and technicians.
- **Industrial/manufacturing:** GE (Aerospace/Vernova), Siemens, Honeywell, Caterpillar, John Deere, 3M, Emerson, Eaton, Schneider Electric.
- **Semiconductors/electronics:** Intel, Micron, Texas Instruments, GlobalFoundries — fabs hire technicians with HS diplomas and are famous for funding associate-degree pipelines with local colleges.
- **Utilities:** almost every regional electric/gas utility has education assistance and technician apprenticeships. Look up *your* utility's careers page.
- **Automotive:** Toyota, Honda, and other manufacturers with US plants; also dealership networks' technician programs.

### Not engineering jobs, but famous education benefits (bridge options)
These can fund school while you apply toward technical roles, and some have internal ladders into maintenance/technical work:
- **Amazon (Career Choice)** — funds degrees/certificates including at community colleges; has a maintenance-tech ladder internally
- **UPS (Earn & Learn)** — part-time package handlers get tuition assistance; part-time-friendly for full-time students
- **Home Depot / Lowe's** — tuition programs; store roles also build real product knowledge in electrical/building trades
- **Starbucks, Chipotle, Target, Walmart** — well-known college programs (often through specific partner schools — check whether *your* school qualifies)

A bridge job at one of these is Plan B, not Plan A: Plan A is a technician job that
teaches the work *and* helps with school. But a bridge job with a real education
benefit still beats a dead-end job with none.

## Apprenticeships: the other way employers pay for education

- **IBEW/NECA electrician apprenticeships** — you're paid to work while the program
  covers your classroom training; journeyman electricians earn a genuinely good
  living, and the credential is portable everywhere. Find your local at ibew.org.
- **Registered apprenticeships** in machining, industrial maintenance, mechatronics:
  search apprenticeship.gov by ZIP code.
- Some manufacturers run their own apprentice programs with local community
  colleges (often called "mechatronics" or "advanced manufacturing" partnerships —
  ask the community college which local employers sponsor students; the college
  knows exactly who pays for what).
