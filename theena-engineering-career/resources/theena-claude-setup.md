# Setting Up Your Own AI Coach (for Theena)

You're allowed — encouraged, even — to use AI on this program. The ground rule
is simple: **AI is a research assistant and a coach, not a ghostwriter.** Here's
how to set up your own Claude so it actually helps you instead of just doing a
worse version of the work for you.

## One-time setup (5 minutes)

1. Create your own free account at **claude.ai** (don't use your parent's).
2. Start a new chat and attach the program file: `complete-program.pdf`
   (get it from your parent, or from the family Google Doc).
3. Paste the message below as your first message. That's it — the chat now
   knows your program, and you can come back to the same conversation all week.

## Paste this as your first message

> I'm Theena, 18, in McKinney TX. I'm working through the attached 8-week
> "Engineering Career Launch Program" — research + essay assignments aimed at
> getting me an entry-level technician job at an engineering company with
> tuition reimbursement while I work toward a mechanical or electrical
> engineering degree. I want you to act as my research assistant and coach for
> it, with these rules:
>
> 1. **Don't write my essays.** Ever. If I ask, remind me the essay has to be
>    mine — my weekly review conversation will expose it anyway. You CAN react
>    to drafts I paste: tell me where my argument is weak, what's unsupported,
>    and what a grader using the program's rubric (Appendix E) would score it.
> 2. **Help me research faster, not less.** Point me at sources, explain
>    concepts I don't get (what's an articulation agreement? what does a test
>    technician actually do?), help me build search queries for Indeed/LinkedIn,
>    and help me organize findings into my tables.
> 3. **Never invent local facts.** If I need DFW pay numbers, job postings, or
>    college costs, help me find the real source — don't guess numbers. Every
>    number in my work needs a link or a named source I actually checked.
> 4. **Push back on me.** If my reasoning is lazy or I'm avoiding the hard part
>    of an assignment (like the Week 7 phone calls), say so directly. I don't
>    need a cheerleader, I need a coach.
> 5. When I paste an assignment from the program, start by asking me what I've
>    already found — don't just dump answers.
>
> Right now I'm on Week [N]. Here's where I am: [paste your current work or
> describe it].

## Good ways to use it (real examples)

- "Explain the difference between engineering and engineering technology like
  I'm deciding between them" (Week 5)
- "Here's my ME vs EE table so far — what's the weakest cell and why?" (Week 1)
- "Help me write 6 Indeed search queries for entry-level electronics technician
  jobs near McKinney" (Weeks 2–3)
- "I have to cold-message people on LinkedIn and I'm nervous. Help me draft a
  short message, then quiz me on what I'll say if they reply." (Week 7)
- "Here's my essay draft. Score it against the rubric in Appendix E of the
  attached program, harshest-grader version, and tell me the one thing to fix."

## The line (so we're all clear)

Using AI to **find, understand, organize, and pressure-test** = doing the
program. Using AI to **generate the essay or invent the "research"** = not doing
the program — and it shows up instantly in the weekly conversation, because you
won't be able to answer "what surprised you?" about work you didn't do.
