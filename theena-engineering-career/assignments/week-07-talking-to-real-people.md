# Week 7 — Talking to Real People

**Goal:** Have real conversations with at least **two people** who do the work —
an engineer, a technician, an apprentice, a community college engineering
instructor, anyone actually on the path. This is the single highest-value week and
the one that most requires leaving your comfort zone. That's not a side effect;
it's the point. Interviews, first days, and asking for raises all live outside the
comfort zone too, and this is the practice version where nothing bad can happen.

## Part 1: Get the conversations

**Finding people (in rough order of ease):**
1. **Family network first.** Parents' friends, friends' parents, neighbors, anyone at church/gym/etc. who works in manufacturing, utilities, construction trades, or engineering. One ask — "do you know anyone who works as an engineer or technician I could talk to for 20 minutes?" — usually produces someone.
2. **Community college.** Email an engineering/engineering-tech instructor: you're deciding between programs and would love 15 minutes. Instructors answer these emails; recruiting interested students is their job.
3. **LinkedIn.** Find technicians/engineers at your Week 3 companies. Short message: who you are, one specific thing about their company from your research, and a request for 15–20 minutes of advice. Send 8–10 to get 2 yeses — low reply rates are normal and not about you.
4. **Walk in.** Small machine shops and repair shops are often genuinely happy to give a curious 18-year-old ten minutes and a tour. Go at a non-busy hour, be brief, be polite.

**The magic words:** people flinch at "can I interview you" but say yes to
*"I'm trying to figure out if this is the right path for me — could I ask you a
few questions about how you got started?"* You are asking for advice, not a job.
People love giving advice.

**Prepare 6–8 questions.** Good ones:
- How did you get your start? What would you do differently at 18?
- What does a normal day actually look like?
- What separates the new hires who move up from the ones who don't?
- Does your company help pay for school? How does that work in practice — do people really use it?
- What should I learn *right now* to be useful on day one?
- Does your company take **interns or co-op students**? Who runs that program?
- Your held-over question from Week 1 — the one the internet couldn't answer
- End with: *"Is there anyone else you think I should talk to?"* (This is how 2 interviews become 4.)

**Afterward:** send a thank-you text/email within 24 hours. Every time. Write notes
immediately — you'll forget the good stuff within a day.

## Part 2: Essay (600–900 words)

**Prompt:** *"What the people doing this job told me."*

Requirements:
- What did each person say that you couldn't have gotten from the internet?
- What surprised you? What contradicted your Weeks 1–5 research? (When a person and a website disagree, that's the most interesting data you have — dig into why.)
- Did anything change your plan? Even "no, and here's why I'm more sure now" counts — but defend it.
- Be honest about the hardest part: reaching out. What did the discomfort feel like before vs. after? Was the anticipation worse than the conversation? (It almost always is. Notice that — it's the most transferable finding of the whole program.)
