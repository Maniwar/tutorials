# Week 5 — Education Pathways: Picking Your Route

**Goal:** Choose, with evidence, how you'd actually get the education — because
"go to school for engineering" hides at least four very different routes with very
different costs, timelines, and lifestyles. Read `resources/education-pathways.md`
first for the map; this week you fill in the local details.

## Part 1: Research

Investigate all four routes **as they actually exist where you live**:

### Route 1: Community college → transfer to a 4-year engineering program
- Which community college(s) near you have an engineering or pre-engineering track?
- Do they have a **transfer agreement (articulation agreement)** with your state university's engineering school? Find the actual agreement page.
- Cost per year, class schedule options (evening? online? — matters if you're working)

### Route 2: Engineering Technology degree (AS or BS in MET/EET)
- What's the difference between *engineering* and *engineering technology*? (Look up both; ask r/AskEngineers)
- Who near you offers it, what does it cost, and what jobs does it lead to?

### Route 3: Apprenticeship
- For EE-leaning: IBEW electrician apprenticeships (paid from day one, "earn while you learn"). Find your local IBEW chapter and their application requirements/timeline.
- For ME-leaning: machinist/toolmaker/industrial maintenance apprenticeships — check apprenticeship.gov for registered programs near you.
- What do apprentices earn in year 1 and at journeyman level in your area?

### Route 4: Straight to a 4-year university
- In-state cost, admission requirements, and what the first two years actually look like (hint: the same calculus/physics you'd take at community college for a third of the price)

**For each route, record:** total cost, years to a credential, whether you can work
while doing it, whether ABET accreditation applies (see the resources file for why
you should care), and what door it opens at the end.

**For every college on your list, also ask** (read
`resources/internships-and-coops.md` first — enrollment is the key that unlocks
internships and co-ops):
- Do you have a **co-op program**? Which local employers take your students?
- Do students get **Handshake** access, and does the career center place pre-engineering/technology students?
- Which employers sponsor or hire your engineering-track students? (The college knows exactly who.)

## Part 2: Essay (700–1000 words)

**Prompt:** *"My education plan and why I chose it."*

Requirements:
- Commit to a route (combinations count — e.g., "technician job + community college nights + transfer" is a route).
- Show your work: why this one over the other three? Use your cost and timeline numbers.
- Name the **first three concrete steps** and put a date on each (e.g., "meet with the community college advisor before ___," "IBEW application window opens ___").
- Name the biggest risk to your plan and what you'd do if it happens.
