# Week 2 — The Hidden Front Door: Jobs Nobody Told You About

**Goal:** Discover the entry-level jobs that put you *inside* engineering companies
without a degree — the roles that come with tuition reimbursement, mentors, and a
ladder. These jobs mostly don't get advertised to 18-year-olds, which is exactly
why most 18-year-olds end up in retail instead.

## The big idea

There is a whole layer of jobs between "cashier" and "engineer":
**technicians, assemblers, drafters, test operators, quality inspectors, field
service techs.** They pay more than retail, they teach you the actual work, the
people around you are engineers, and many employers will pay for your degree while
you work. Read `resources/entry-level-jobs.md` in this folder first — it's your
starting vocabulary.

## Part 1: Research

Build a list of **10 entry-level job titles** relevant to ME or EE. For each one:

1. **Job title** (as it actually appears on Indeed/LinkedIn — exact words matter for searching)
2. **What you'd do all day** (2–3 sentences, from real postings, not guesses)
3. **Typical pay in our area** (from actual local postings — record the range and the source posting)
4. **Requirements** (high school diploma? certificate? Which ones say "no experience necessary"?)
5. **Where it leads** (what does this job promote into?)

**Where to look:**
- Indeed / LinkedIn / ZipRecruiter — search the exact titles from `resources/entry-level-jobs.md`, filtered to your area
- Search tricks: `"no experience" technician`, `"entry level" manufacturing`, `"will train"`
- Local temp/staffing agencies that place in manufacturing (Aerotek, Adecco, Manpower) — their listings show what's really hiring

**Rules:**
- At least 5 of the 10 must be jobs you'd never heard of before this week.
- At least 3 must explicitly require no experience or say "will train."
- Every pay number needs a link or screenshot of the posting it came from.

## Part 2: Essay (600–900 words)

**Prompt:** *"The jobs nobody told me about."*

Requirements:
- Pick the **3 titles from your list you'd most want**, ranked, and defend the ranking.
- Compare the best of them, honestly, to the jobs you've been taking: pay, what you learn, where each one is likely to put you in 3 years. Use real numbers.
- Address this question head-on: *"If these jobs are better, why do most people my age not get them?"* (Hint: it's usually not ability.)
